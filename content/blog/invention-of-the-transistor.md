# The Invention of the Transistor

On December 23, 1947, John Bardeen and Walter Brattain demonstrated at Bell Labs a device that amplified an electrical signal using a point of indium pressed against a germanium crystal. They called it the point-contact transistor. It was fragile, temperamental, and barely better than the vacuum tubes it was meant to replace — but it proved that a solid could do what a hot glass bulb had done for decades.

## The vacuum tube problem

By the late 1940s, electronic computers were already being built around vacuum tubes. ENIAC, completed in 1946, used roughly 18,000 of them. Tubes were large, consumed enormous power, ran hot, and failed often; operators spent hours hunting down the tube that had just burned out. The Bell Labs research group led by William Shockley saw the transistor as a way to make electronics smaller, cooler, cheaper, and more reliable.

## From theory to practice

The idea began with theory. In 1948, Bardeen and Brattain published a paper explaining how a semiconductor surface could control the flow of current, and they built working point-contact devices that same year. Shockley, working in parallel, imagined a cleaner design: a semiconductor with two electrical contacts separated by a thin region of different material. The result, unveiled in June 1948, was the junction transistor — a device that could be manufactured consistently rather than tuned by hand.

The three men shared the 1956 Nobel Prize in Physics for their work.

## Why it mattered

The transistor did not just improve electronics; it changed what was possible. Because transistors could be made small and cheap, engineers could put thousands of them on a single chip — the integrated circuit, first demonstrated by Jack Kilby in 1958 and Robert Noyce in 1959. Every computer, phone, and appliance since has been a descendant of that chain: the transistor made the integrated circuit possible, and the integrated circuit made the microprocessor possible.

Without the transistor, the computer that fits in your pocket would have needed a room, a cooling system, and a maintenance crew.
